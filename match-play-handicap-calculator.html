How it's calculated

Start from each player's Course Handicap for the tees being played, unrounded if you work it out from a Handicap Index (Index × Slope ÷ 113 + (Course Rating − Par)). Then apply the allowance for the format and round once, to the nearest whole number (.5 up; a plus handicap moves toward zero):

  • Singles match play, 100%: the higher player receives the difference in Course Handicap. The lower player plays off 0.
  • Four-ball (4BBB, best-ball) match play, 90%: each player receives 90% of the difference between their Course Handicap and the lowest in the group. Since 2024 the 90% applies to the difference, not to each handicap first.
  • Foursomes match play, 50%: the side with the higher combined Course Handicap receives half the difference between the two combined totals.
  • Four-ball stroke play or Stableford, 85%: each player gets 85% of their own Course Handicap. Nobody plays off the low player.

Strokes fall on the holes with the lowest stroke index: 8 strokes go on stroke index 1 to 8. A player receiving 19 gets one on every hole and a second on stroke index 1. In stroke play a plus player gives strokes back from stroke index 18.

Worked example

This is the four-ball example in Appendix C (C/2). Course Handicaps are A 10, B 18, C 27 and D 39. A has the lowest, so A plays off 0 and the others take 90% of the difference from 10:

  • B: 90% × 8 = 7.2, so 7 strokes, on stroke index 1 to 7
  • C: 90% × 17 = 15.3, so 15 strokes
  • D: 90% × 29 = 26.1, so 26 strokes: one on every hole and a second on stroke index 1 to 8

Singles A v B: B receives 8. Foursomes A+B v C+D: 50% × (66 − 28) = 19 for C and D.

Under the pre-2024 method (90% of each handicap, then the difference), players of 11 and 16 got 4 strokes; now it is 90% × 5 = 4.5, so 5. Tap Load example at the top to see the four-ball version with its stroke table.

Rules sources, checked 28 Nov 2026: USGA, Rules of Handicapping, Appendix C: Handicap Allowances, with Clarifications C/2 and C/3 (effective 1 January 2024); The R&A, Appendix C (the same table and examples); USGA, Four-Ball Match Play (90%) and Foursomes Match Play; USGA, 2024 WHS Reference for Clubs; Golf Australia, WHS Official reference guide for Australian administrators, Operational Guidance & Supporting Regulations, Edition January 2026, §10(A)–(C).

Frequently asked questions

How many strokes do you get in match play?

In singles match play the higher handicap receives the full difference in Course Handicap (100% allowance) and the lower player plays off 0. With Course Handicaps of 10 and 18, the 18 receives 8 strokes, on stroke index 1 to 8 (Rules of Handicapping, Appendix C, checked 28 Nov 2026).

How do four-ball (4BBB) match play handicaps work since 2024?

Work out each player's Course Handicap, take the difference from the lowest in the group, then apply 90% and round. Before 2024 the 90% was applied to each handicap first. With 11 and 16 the difference is 5, 90% is 4.5, so 5 strokes; the old way gave 4. Source: USGA 2024 WHS Reference for Clubs and Appendix C, checked 28 Nov 2026.

How are foursomes match play strokes worked out?

Add each side's Course Handicaps. The side with the higher total receives 50% of the difference, rounded once; the other side plays off 0. Course Handicaps 10 + 18 against 27 + 39 give 28 against 66, so the second side receives 19 strokes (Appendix C, C/2 Example 4).

What allowance is used in four-ball stroke play or Stableford?

Each player gets 85% of their own Course Handicap, rounded. It is not worked out from the low player, because it is a stroke-play format. Course Handicaps of +4, 16, 7 and 26 become +3, 14, 6 and 22 (Appendix C, C/3).

Should Australians enter their Daily Handicap?

No. A Golf Australia Daily Handicap already includes the 0.93 stroke-play multiplier and the consistency factor. Enter your GA Handicap with the Slope, Scratch Rating and Par (choose Index), or a Course Handicap before that multiplier. Golf Australia's guide (January 2026, §10) recommends the same match play allowances: 100% individual, 90% four-ball and 50% of combined for foursomes.

What happens to a plus handicap in match play?

In match play the lowest handicap plays off 0 and everyone else receives the difference, so a plus player simply plays off 0 and the others get the extra strokes. In stroke play a plus player gives strokes back to the course, starting at stroke index 18. Allowances move a plus handicap toward zero, including when rounding.

Do you round the Course Handicap before applying the allowance?

No. The allowance is applied to the unrounded Course Handicap and the result is rounded once (Rule 6.2a/1). With the USGA's worked-example tees (Slope 135, Course Rating 72.3, Par 72), Indexes of 8.4 and 11.4 give Course Handicaps of 10.335 and 13.919. In four-ball, 90% of the 3.584 difference is 3.23, so 3 strokes; rounding the Course Handicaps to 10 and 14 first would give 4.

Where do the strokes fall if I receive more than 18?

One on every hole, then a second on the holes with the lowest stroke index. A player receiving 19 gets two strokes on stroke index 1; 26 strokes means two on stroke index 1 to 8. For 9 holes, strokes go on the 9 holes in stroke-index order.
