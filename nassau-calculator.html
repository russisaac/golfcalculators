    How it's calculated

    A Nassau is three bets at the same stake: the front nine, the back nine and the overall 18. Each is a match (holes won, halves allowed) or, if you choose aggregate, the lower total score for those holes. In 2 v 2 better ball, each side's score on a hole is the better of the two partners' scores, gross or net.

    • Automatic press (on by default): when a side goes 2 down in a bet, a new bet at the same stake starts on the next hole and runs to the end of that nine. Each bet starts at most one automatic press, and a press can itself be pressed.
    • No press starts when the trigger is on the last hole of a nine. The overall 18 is not auto-pressed unless you switch it on under More options.
    • The trailing side can also start a press by hand on any hole left in the nine.
    • Net: strokes come off the low player, 90% of the difference for 2 v 2 (the four-ball match play allowance) and 100% for 1 v 1, then go on the holes with the lowest stroke index.

    Every bet won is worth the stake to the winning side. In 2 v 2 the side's result is split equally between partners, then the payments are settled in the fewest transfers, the same way as our Skins calculator.

    Worked example

    A $5 Nassau, 1 v 1, gross, with automatic presses. Alex wins holes 1 to 4, Blake 5 to 7, Alex 8 and 9. Alex is 2 up after hole 2, so a press starts on hole 3; Alex is 2 up on that press after hole 4, so a second press starts on hole 5; Blake goes 2 up on that one after hole 6, so a third starts on hole 7.

    • Front: Alex 3 up, +$5. Press 3–9: Alex 1 up, +$5. Press 5–9: Blake 1 up, −$5. Press 7–9: Alex 1 up, +$5.
    • Back: Blake wins 10 and 11, the rest are halved. Blake 2 up, −$5 for Alex; the press from 12 is halved.
    • Overall: Alex 1 up, +$5.

    Alex +$10.00, Blake −$10.00: Blake pays Alex $10.00.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a Nassau in golf?

    A Nassau is three bets in one round at the same stake: the front nine, the back nine and the overall 18. A $5 Nassau puts up to $15 at stake before any presses. It is a betting game with house rules, not part of the Rules of Golf.

    How does a 2-down automatic press work?

    When a side goes 2 down in a bet, a new bet at the same stake starts on the next hole and runs to the end of that nine. Each bet starts at most one automatic press, and none starts if the trigger is on the 9th or 18th. This is the usual convention (28 Nov 2026), so agree it on the first tee.

    Can you press a press?

    Yes. A press is a bet in its own right, so if a side goes 2 down on a press, that press starts its own press on the next hole. Presses can chain like this to the end of the nine.

    Is the overall 18 pressed too?

    Not by default: automatic presses run on the front and back nines only. Switch on “Auto-press the overall 18” under More options if your group presses the overall as well; those presses run to the 18th.

    What is the difference between 1 v 1 and 2 v 2 better ball?

    In 1 v 1 each player plays their own ball against the other. In 2 v 2 better ball, each side counts the better score of its two partners on every hole. Winnings and losses are split equally between partners and then settled in the fewest payments.

    Match play or aggregate?

    Match play counts holes won, so a blow-up hole only loses that hole. Aggregate counts total strokes for the nine or the 18, so every shot matters. With aggregate, “2 down” for an automatic press means 2 strokes behind.

    How are handicaps used in a net Nassau?

    Strokes come off the low player. For 2 v 2 better ball the calculator uses the four-ball match play allowance, 90% of the difference in Course Handicap from the lowest player; for 1 v 1, 100% of the difference (Rules of Handicapping, Appendix C, checked 28 Nov 2026). In Australia, enter each player's Daily Handicap. You can change the allowance under More options.

    Is a Nassau in the Rules of Golf?

    No. The Rules of Golf and the Rules of Handicapping cover match play and handicap allowances, but the Nassau, its stakes and its presses are a side game played to whatever your group agrees. This calculator follows the common conventions and shows every bet so you can check it.
