    How it's calculated

    Skins has no governing rulebook, so this calculator uses the common club format. Each hole is worth one skin and the lowest score wins it outright. If the lowest score is tied, the skin carries to the next hole. Each skin is worth the stake from every other player.

    Worked example

    Four players play for $5 a skin, net, with full handicaps and carryovers on. Their Daily Handicaps are Alex 0, Blake 4, Casey 8 and Drew 12. Strokes go by stroke index (SI), so Drew gets one stroke on SI 1–12, Casey on SI 1–8 and Blake on SI 1–4.

    • Hole 1 (par 4, SI 5): the nets are Alex 4, Blake 5, Casey 3 and Drew 4. Casey wins 1 skin.
    • Holes 2–4: two or more players tie for the low net on each, so 3 skins carry.
    • Hole 5 (par 4, SI 9) is the carryover hole. Drew scores 3, gets a stroke and nets 2, the only low net. He wins 4 skins: hole 5 plus the 3 carried.

    After 18 holes Casey has 2 skins and Drew 13. 3 skins are still carried after the last hole, so nobody wins them. Each balance is $5 × (4 × the player's skins − 15 skins won):

    Alex −$75.00 · Blake −$75.00 · Casey −$35.00 · Drew +$185.00

    Alex pays Drew $75.00, Blake pays Drew $75.00 and Casey pays Drew $35.00. Tap Load example at the top to see every hole.

    Rules source: no governing body publishes skins rules, so this is the common club format. As with any competition, your group decides before the round how tied holes and leftover skins are settled (R&A Rules of Golf, Committee Procedures 5A(6)).

    Frequently asked questions

    How do carryovers work in a skins game?

    If two or more players tie for the lowest score on a hole, nobody wins that skin and it carries to the next hole, which is then worth two skins. Carries keep adding up until one player wins a hole outright. You can switch carryovers off under More options.

    Should we play net or gross skins?

    Gross skins compare actual strokes, which suits players of similar ability. Net skins take handicap strokes off first, by stroke index, so higher handicaps can win holes. Off the low gives each player the difference between their handicap and the lowest in the group. Agree on one before you tee off.

    What happens to skins still carried after the 18th?

    Your group decides before the round. Common choices are that nobody wins them, or that a playoff, such as a chip-off or an extra hole, decides who takes them all. Both are under More options, and the calculator asks for the playoff winner when one is needed.

    How much should a skin be worth?

    Whatever your group agrees. Remember that each skin is paid by every other player: at $5 a skin in a four-ball, winning one skin collects $15. Small stakes keep it social, and the calculator handles any amount.

    How are skins payouts split?

    Each player's balance is the stake × (number of players × their skins − total skins won), so the balances always add up to $0. The calculator then lists who pays whom, matching the biggest debts with the biggest winnings so the group needs only a few payments.

    How are handicap strokes given in net skins?

    Players get one stroke on each hole whose stroke index is at or below their Daily Handicap, and a second on the hardest holes if it is over 18. A plus handicap gives strokes back from stroke index 18. For 9 holes, use each player's 9-hole Daily Handicap (from the Daily Handicap calculator), not half the 18-hole one.
